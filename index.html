Threat Briefing

What Is Phishing?

Phishing is a social engineering attack where a message pretends to be trustworthy so it can trick you into clicking a link, opening a file, giving away credentials, or sending money. The attacker is not breaking encryption directly. They are trying to manipulate your judgment.

Common Signs
  • Urgent pressure like "act now" or "your account will close"
  • Suspicious sender names or domains that almost match a real company
  • Shortened or strange links that hide the real destination
  • Requests for passwords, payment details, or document downloads
  • Odd grammar, inconsistent branding, or unexpected attachments
Best Response
  • Pause before clicking and inspect the sender carefully
  • Hover over links and verify the true destination
  • Use official websites or apps instead of the email link
  • Report suspicious messages to your team or provider
  • When in doubt, verify through a second channel

Defense Briefing

How Password Guessing Works

A computer does not "understand" your password. It just tries huge numbers of combinations very quickly. The longer the password is, and the more character types it can contain, the larger that search space becomes.

Length

Every extra character multiplies the number of guesses needed, which is why longer passwords matter so much.

Character Set

Lowercase only is much smaller than upper and lowercase together, and adding numbers or symbols expands it further.
